The main site

10-12 Ballow Road should be judged as public-facing gateway infrastructure.

The case is not “build anything at any cost”. The case is that this particular address sits where the ferry, town centre, visitor flow, future terminal upgrade and community need already meet.

What is known

The site has unusual leverage, but no magic exemption.

The public property listing describes 10-12 Ballow Road as a 7,727 square metre freehold waterfront development site, owned by the State of Queensland, near ferry services and local amenities. It also says future development is subject to statutory approvals.

That is exactly why the public proposal needs to be careful. A community-led hub must answer planning, cultural, access, traffic, flooding, parking, maintenance, safety, insurance and economic questions before anyone treats the idea as settled.

Why Dunwich / Goompi

This is where ordinary access becomes the design brief.

The $41 million Dunwich ferry terminal upgrade is already looking at arrival, safety, accessibility, wayfinding, commercial opportunities and cultural/heritage values. A nearby hub should help those goals feel local, not generic.

Arrival

Visitors can be welcomed into useful island information before they disappear into cars and buses.

Movement

Markets, sport and cinema need last-return ferry notes, bus clarity, safe paths and realistic parking.

Everyday use

If locals do not use it on normal days, it will not earn trust during events or emergencies.

What this page does not claim

No approval, no ownership claim, no secret fait accompli.

A sceptical reader should not have to guess the boundary. This is a public concept site for discussion and partner-readiness, not proof that the address has been acquired or approved.
