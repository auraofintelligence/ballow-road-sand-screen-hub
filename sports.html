Sand sports

Sand sport does not have to live on the beach.

Permanent, managed courts can be safer, cheaper to maintain, easier to store gear beside, less exposed to wind, and more reliable than relying on shifting beaches.

The practical argument

If grass fields are underused, sand can add new life without pretending to replace every sport.

The proposal starts from a simple observation: everything that needs a full grass oval is already competing for limited use. Sand courts create different activity: volleyball, beach cricket, footvolley, beach tennis, flag football, beach 5s, ultimate and low-pressure family games.

Managed inland sand means fixed sockets, storage, lighting, shade, booking, drainage, safer surfaces, access paths and equipment that does not vanish with the next storm tide.

Activity mix

A portfolio, not one code trying to own the space.

Different groups need different doors. A teen might start with spikeball, a retired couple with beach tennis, a tourist group with social volleyball, a school group with flag football, and a local club with beach cricket.

Olympic anchors

Beach volleyball and future-facing flag football give the project a 2032-friendly spine.

Local fun

Beach cricket, ultimate and casual tournaments keep the mood Straddie rather than elite-only.

Older bodies

Beach tennis, walking games, mobility loops and shaded viewing make the site useful for older residents too.

Why not just beaches?

Beaches are beautiful; they are not a storage shed, club room, booking system or insurance plan.

Beach pop-ups are still useful. But permanent club development needs stability: fixtures, drainage, shade, toilets, lighting, risk controls, maintenance and a place to keep the gear.

Wind

Less exposure

Inland courts can be sheltered by trees, buildings and careful orientation.

Gear

Storage nearby

Posts, nets, balls, first aid, water and shade should not depend on car boots.

Coast

Lower risk

Fixtures should not sit where erosion, storms or shifting beach profiles can erase them.

Access

More inclusive

Paths, seating, toilets and drop-off points help people who cannot wander across soft beach sand.
